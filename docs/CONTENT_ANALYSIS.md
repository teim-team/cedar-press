## The one-paragraph version

Three of the four corpora now carry measured, per-year content series. The two
findings worth quoting are that **gaming has fallen from 14.4% to 5.7% of what
Indian Country lobbies about** while public safety tripled, and that **the last
three years produced more NAGPRA notices (1,996) than the entire first
seventeen years of the corpus (1,925)**. The two findings worth acting on
internally are that **only about 13% of the 156,452-document Federal Register
corpus is actually about Indian Country**, and that **our own theme classifier
invented a NAGPRA collapse between 2003 and 2010 that never happened** — an
artefact of abstract availability that would have shipped as a finding.

Every classifier below carries an audited error rate. Where the data does not
support a question, it is listed in [What the data does not
support](#what-the-data-does-not-support) rather than answered weakly.

**Two things about these numbers that matter more than their size.**

*Exact-set match is a deliberately brutal metric.* A filing coded
`land_water|gaming` against a hand label of `land_water|gaming|sovereignty` is
scored a total failure. Per-label precision and recall of ~0.84 are the fairer
description: when the classifier names a family, it is right about five times in
six. Use the label metrics for judging a family's series; use exact-set match
for judging whether a single filing's full label is trustworthy (it often is
not).

*The raw and corpus-weighted relevance numbers differ because the sample is
stratified.* It deliberately oversamples the small tiers — 40 of 9,523
`title_subject` documents but 25 of 132,964 `body_only`. The 75.8% describes the
sample; the **91.8% describes the corpus** and is the number to cite.
Per-stratum detail: `fr_relevance_stratum_audit.csv`.

## 1. What Indian Country lobbies about, and how it has changed

**The federal disclosure system cannot answer this question, which is why the
content analysis exists.** The LDA gives filers a 73-code issue taxonomy. For
this population it collapses: **18,403 of 22,699 coded filings carry `IND`**
(Indian / Native American affairs). A tribe lobbying on broadband, a tribe
lobbying on water rights and a tribe lobbying on casino regulation all file
under the same code. The free-text `specific_issues_text` is the only place the
actual subject appears.

Classification runs on **issue segments, not filings**. Filers write one issue
per line and repeat the whole block across activity records separated by `|||`;
classifying the concatenated blob would count the same line up to nine times and
would mark a filing "energy" because one of nine lines mentioned a pipeline.

### The central series — and the trap under it

The first version of this series measured each family's **share of classified
filings** and showed almost every family rising. That was wrong. The mean number
of issue families disclosed per filing rose from ~2.0 in 2002–03 to ~3.1 by the
2020s (`lobbying_disclosure_verbosity_year.csv`) — filings got wordier, so
against that denominator everything grows. It would have produced a page of
spurious growth findings.

The verbosity-neutral measure is **composition**: each family's share of all
family mentions that year. That is the series to quote, and it is the
`share_of_family_mentions` column in `lobbying_issue_family_year.csv`.

| Issue family | 1999–2007 | 2018–2026 | Change |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| **gaming** | **14.4%** | **5.7%** | **−8.7pp** |
| land_water | 14.7% | 12.0% | −2.7pp |
| transportation | 5.8% | 3.1% | −2.8pp |
| education | 3.3% | 2.6% | −0.6pp |
| energy | 5.3% | 4.6% | −0.6pp |
| appropriations | 14.3% | 13.8% | −0.5pp |
| health | 8.1% | 8.3% | +0.2pp |
| taxation | 4.6% | 5.1% | +0.5pp |
| econ_dev_contracting | 4.8% | 5.3% | +0.5pp |
| social_services | 1.6% | 2.0% | +0.5pp |
| housing | 2.7% | 3.7% | +1.0pp |
| broadband | 0.5% | 1.6% | +1.2pp |
| sovereignty_governance | 9.9% | 11.4% | +1.6pp |
| culture_repatriation | 0.7% | 2.6% | +1.9pp |
| agriculture | 0.4% | 2.4% | +2.0pp |
| environment_nr | 7.0% | 9.3% | +2.3pp |
| **public_safety** | **2.0%** | **6.2%** | **+4.2pp** |

**The findings that survive the verbosity control:**

1. **Gaming has more than halved as a share of tribal lobbying**, 14.4% → 5.7%.
   It was the second-largest family in the early period and is now seventh. This
   is the single largest movement in the table and it survives every control
   applied.
2. **Public safety tripled**, 2.0% → 6.2% — consistent with the VAWA
   reauthorisation fights, tribal-jurisdiction litigation and MMIW legislation
   of the 2010s and 2020s.
3. **Culture and repatriation, and agriculture, each roughly tripled** off very
   small bases.

**A finding that does NOT survive, and was expected to:** the hypothesis that
lobbying shifted *from gaming toward broadband and health*. Broadband did rise
(0.5% → 1.6%), but it remains one of the two smallest families. **Health is
flat** (8.1% → 8.3%). The gaming share did not move to health. Reported here
because it was the prior, and it is not supported.

### How wrong is this classifier

Exact-set match 60.0%, label precision 0.84, label recall 0.84 (n=120).

**Dominant error mode: `land_water` is under-detected — 23 of the 44 total
misses.** The pattern covers "land into trust", "fee-to-trust", "water rights",
but not the many other ways filers write it: *land conveyance*, *land
exchange*, *land transfer*, *trust land applications*, *land status*,
*Reservation Reaffirmation Act*. The `land_water` series is therefore a
**floor**, and its −2.7pp change is the least trustworthy row in the table.
This is the first thing to fix in a v2.

Over-fires are milder and spread across `appropriations` (7),
`sovereignty_governance` (6) and `environment_nr` (6), typically where a bill
title contains an incidental word.

**Non-disclosure is itself a measurement.** Of 27,796 filings, **8,324 disclose
no issue text at all**, and a further 2,102 segments are pure boilerplate
("General tribal issues", "Monitor Indian related legislation", "Federal
Representation"). These are counted and published in the `text_status` column
rather than silently dropped: about **30% of filings tell you nothing** about
what was lobbied on, and that is a fact about the LDA regime worth stating.

**A client-universe caveat surfaced by the audit.** Several sampled filings are
not tribal entities at all — Coeur d'Alene **Mines** Corporation (a mining
company, not the Coeur d'Alene Tribe), Salt River Project (a public utility),
the Metropolitan Water District, the City of Santa Rosa. These are name
collisions in the client net upstream of this analysis. They are flagged in the
audit file's `note` column. This work does not change client attribution — a
concurrent agent owns entity keys — but the lobbying client universe needs a
precision pass.

## 2. What the federal government does, by type

### The thing to know before quoting any FR number

**The 156,452-document corpus is a full-text keyword net, and about 85% of it is
not about Indian Country.** 153,143 of the rows were caught by a body-text
keyword match. A document enters if the word "tribal" appears anywhere in it.

We hold `title`, `abstract`, `action` and `dates` — **not body text**. So for a
document whose only Native term is in the body, we have neither the means to
classify it nor the means to verify it belongs. Documents are therefore tiered
by the strength of the available evidence (`fr_content_classification.csv`):

| Tier | Documents | Share | Meaning |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| `title_subject` | 9,523 | 6.1% | Native term in the **title** |
| `abstract_subject` | 12,855 | 8.2% | Native term in the abstract only |
| `weak_term_only` | 1,110 | 0.7% | Only ambiguous terms (*reservation*, *Pueblo*) |
| `body_only_unverifiable` | 132,964 | 85.0% | **No Native term in any field we hold** |

Hand-coding a stratified sample of 120 and reweighting to the corpus gives:

- **Corpus-weighted accuracy 91.8%**, precision 0.68, recall 0.73.
- **Estimated 20,842 documents (13.3% of the corpus) are genuinely about Indian
  Country** — not 156,452.

**Never publish "156,452 federal actions affecting tribal nations."** The audit
sample makes the reason concrete: `body_only` documents drawn at random include
*Establishment of the Waco Mammoth National Monument*, *Safety Zone; Hilo
Harbor*, *US Savings Bonds Series I*, and *Chromium Electroplating Emission
Standards*.

The tier rule's own failures are equally instructive and are published in
`content_audit_fr_relevance.csv`:

- **False positives from place names.** *Indian Point Nuclear Generating Unit
  No. 3* (New York), *Radio Broadcasting Services; Indian Wells, CA*, *Indian
  River Lagoon South Project* (Florida), *Oak Ridge Reservation* (a DOE site).
  The `abstract_subject` tier is the weak one — barely half its sampled
  documents are genuinely tribal-subject, because "State, Tribal, and Federal
  agencies are invited to comment" is boilerplate in thousands of notices.
- **False negatives that matter more.** *Proclaiming Certain Lands ... as an
  addition to the Pueblo of Laguna Reservation* (a fee-to-trust action),
  *Transfer of Excess Property — Cherokee Nation*, the *Osage Negotiated
  Rulemaking Committee*, and the Four Corners / Navajo Generating Station air
  rules. All are squarely Indian Country and all were missed.

### Theme series

Themes are assigned only to the 22,378 classifiable documents.
`fr_theme_year.csv` carries the per-year counts and shares. Exact-set match
70.0%, label precision 0.85, recall 0.84 (n=110).

Error modes are symmetric and mostly boundary disputes between
`environment_nr`, `energy_minerals` and `water_rights` — a document about a
coal-fired plant's air permit on a reservation legitimately belongs to more than
one. **A genuine vocabulary gap, not a classifier error:** there is no theme for
**land administration, leasing and rights-of-way**. *Rights-of-Way on Indian
Land* — a major BIA final rule — has no home in the vocabulary. Add it in v2.

### The NAGPRA artefact — the most important internal finding here

The theme series showed NAGPRA notices collapsing from ~80/yr in 2002 to ~17/yr
across 2003–2010, then exploding after 2011. **That did not happen.**

Before 2011, almost no NAGPRA notice in this corpus carries an abstract at all
(0–5 per year, against 90–214 notices per year). Their titles — *"Notice of
Inventory Completion: Beloit College, Logan Museum of Anthropology, Beloit,
WI"* — contain no Native term. So they fall into `body_only_unverifiable`, never
reach the theme classifier, and vanish from the series. The trough is an
artefact of abstract availability interacting with our own relevance tier. It
was caught because one such notice turned up in the hand-audit sample
(`content_audit_fr_relevance.csv`, the Connecticut State Museum notice).

The correction does not re-tune the tier rule — that would invalidate the audit.
It classifies NAGPRA on the signal present throughout: **the FR's own
standardised notice titles**, prescribed by 43 CFR part 10. *"Notice of Inventory
Completion"* **is** a NAGPRA notice; precision is near-total and it does not
depend on an abstract existing.

**Use `fr_nagpra_title_index_year.csv` for NAGPRA over time. Do not use the
theme series for it.**

> **Filename note.** These outputs were first written to `nagpra_notices.csv`
> and silently clobbered a concurrent agent's much richer NAGPRA build
> (institution parsing, MNI counts, resolved entity IDs). It was caught only
> because a row count read back 1,180 against the 6,606 written. Ours is now
> `fr_nagpra_title_index*` and is an **index of the parent FR corpus**, not the
> NAGPRA product — `nagpra_notices.csv` is that, and it is the better artefact
> for the subset cut. Do not rename ours back.

| | Notices |
|---|---:|
| Total NAGPRA notices, 1994–2026 | **6,606** |
| Missed by the relevance tier rule | 1,249 (18.9%) |
| 1994–2010 (seventeen years) | **1,925** |
| 2023–2025 (three years) | **1,996** |

**The last three years produced more NAGPRA notices than the first seventeen
combined** — 496 (2023), 670 (2024), 830 (2025). This is a real surge, and it is
consistent with the revised NAGPRA regulations at 43 CFR part 10 effective
January 2024, which required institutions to complete and update inventories.
This is the strongest single subset story in the corpus and it confirms the
recommendation in `SUBSET_DATASETS.md` to cut NAGPRA first.

**The generalisation:** abstract coverage runs 70–82% before 2011 and 88–92%
after (`fr_abstract_availability_year.csv`). **Every pre-2011 theme count is a
floor, not a level, and no theme's trend may be read across the 2011 boundary
without checking whether it depends on abstracts.**

## 3. Which agencies engage, and where advocacy is aimed

Two vocabularies that do not agree — FR says *"Interior Department; Indian
Affairs Bureau"*, LDA says *"Interior, Dept of (DOI)"* — are normalised to a
deliberately coarse department label. A finer crosswalk would imply a precision
neither source supports. `agency_attention_vs_advocacy.csv` and its `_year`
companion.

**Congress is excluded from the comparison.** It is 42.3% of lobbying targets
and 0.0% of FR documents, because Congress does not publish in the Federal
Register. That gap measures the two sources' definitions, not anybody's
behaviour. Quote the `*_executive_only` columns.

| Department | FR docs | FR share | Lobbying filings | Lobbying share | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Interior | 12,524 | 56.3% | 12,334 | 45.2% | −11.2pp |
| White House / EOP | 106 | 0.5% | 2,002 | 7.3% | **+6.9pp** |
| EPA | 1,795 | 8.1% | 800 | 2.9% | **−5.1pp** |
| Justice | 197 | 0.9% | 1,022 | 3.7% | +2.9pp |
| Treasury | 210 | 0.9% | 1,039 | 3.8% | +2.9pp |
| Defense | 177 | 0.8% | 877 | 3.2% | +2.4pp |
| Education | 659 | 3.0% | 198 | 0.7% | −2.2pp |
| HUD | 879 | 4.0% | 508 | 1.9% | −2.1pp |
| Agriculture | 610 | 2.7% | 1,302 | 4.8% | +2.0pp |

**The interesting part is the sign pattern.** Where the government *acts* by
regulation — EPA, Education, HUD, Interior itself — advocacy is aimed
proportionally less. Where advocacy concentrates — the Executive Office of the
President, Justice, Treasury, Defense — comparatively little tribal-subject
regulation is published. That is consistent with lobbying targeting
**discretionary and appropriations channels** rather than notice-and-comment
rulemaking: you lobby OMB and Treasury about money and tax treatment, and you
comment on EPA rules through a different door.

**Caveats that bound this.** (a) The two denominators are different objects — a
document with three issuing agencies versus a filing naming eight targets;
shares are comparable, raw counts are not. (b) FR agency attribution is exact
(the FR states its issuing agencies) while lobbying targets are filer-reported
and unaudited. (c) The FR side inherits the relevance tier's precision of 0.68,
so agency shares carry that error. This table supports a **directional** claim
about where attention and advocacy diverge; it does not support a precise
elasticity.

## 4. The consultation record

**This measure was wrong on the first pass and the correction is the finding.**
A naive rule counted 2,795 "consultation notices". Nearly all were NAGPRA
inventory notices containing the sentence *"in consultation with the appropriate
Indian Tribes or Native Hawaiian organizations"* — which is evidence that
consultation **happened**, but is not a notice announcing one. Publishing the
two together would have inflated the consultation record roughly six-fold.

Two measures, published separately and **never summed**:

| Measure | Count | Meaning |
|---|---:|---|
| `fr_consultation_notices.csv` | **484** | The document's purpose is to convene or announce consultation (263 by title signal) |
| `fr_consultation_referenced.csv` | 1,829 | The document reports consultation already undertaken |

**Who consults** (`fr_consultation_by_agency.csv`): Interior 151, HHS 99, EPA
43, Commerce 30, Energy 23, SBA 17, HUD 14, Labor 13, Transportation 13. That
Interior is under a third of consultation notices, with HHS and EPA together
nearly matching it, is the useful shape here — consultation is meaningfully
government-wide even though tribal-subject *rulemaking* is 56% Interior.

**Volume over time** (`fr_consultation_year.csv`): notices run 6–20/yr through
the 2000s, peak at 34 in 2012 and 27 in 2023, and fall to 7 and 6 in 2025–2026.
**Do not read the 2025–26 decline as a policy finding** — the corpus is
incomplete for 2026, and the notice count is small enough that single-digit
moves are noise.

**The `referenced` series must not be read as a trend at all.** It is 0 before
2011 purely because pre-2011 abstracts are missing, and it collapses to ~1 in
2024–25 because the NAGPRA notice template changed wording. It measures template
language, not consultation practice. It is published because it is useful for
retrieval, and flagged here because its shape is entirely artefactual.

**Consultation notices do not track policy volume.** Classifiable tribal-subject
FR documents roughly doubled from ~650/yr to ~1,300/yr between 2020 and 2024,
while consultation notices stayed flat in the teens and twenties. On the
available evidence the two series are unrelated. That is a real answer to the
question asked, and the direction is worth stating: **the consultation record
did not grow with the policy record.**

## What the data does not support

Listed explicitly, because a weak answer is worse than a stated gap.

1. **"156,452 federal actions affecting tribal nations."** ~13% of the corpus,
   about 20,800 documents, is the defensible figure. The rest cannot be
   verified from the fields we hold.
2. **Content classification of the 132,964 `body_only` documents.** We do not
   hold body text. This is not a modelling problem; it is a missing-data
   problem. Fixing it means re-pulling full text from the FR API — feasible, not
   free, and blocked today by pull discipline.
3. **Any pre-2011 FR theme level or trend.** Abstract coverage changes under the
   series. Pre-2011 counts are floors. NAGPRA has a corrected series; no other
   theme does.
4. **The lobbying `land_water` trend.** Recall failure on that family is the
   classifier's worst defect (23 of 44 misses). The level is a floor and the
   change is unreliable.
5. **Any per-filing lobbying label.** Exact-set match is 60%. The series are
   sound in aggregate; an individual filing's family list is not a fact to
   publish about that client.
6. **Dollar amounts attached to any issue family.** `spend_usd` is a filing-level
   total covering every issue in the filing. It cannot be apportioned across
   families, and any "tribes spent $X lobbying on gaming" number would be
   fabricated. Not computed here, and should not be.
7. **Who consults whom, at entity level.** Consultation notices name the agency
   reliably; they do not reliably name which tribes participated, and no
   entity attribution was performed (a concurrent agent owns entity keys).
8. **The 2026 partial year.** Present in every series and complete in none.
   Excluded from all window comparisons above.
9. **The ANC annual reports (246 files, 29.4 MB).** No classifier was built.
   They are OCR'd corporate annual reports already mined for a specific purpose
   (28 deal rows in `deals_anc_reports_additions.csv`), they carry OCR noise, and
   there is no subscriber question that a theme count over them answers. The one
   defensible cut — extracting subsidiary and business-segment names to feed the
   ownership graph — is a named-entity task with its own accuracy burden, not a
   content classification, and is left as scoped future work rather than
   attempted weakly.
10. **The bills corpus (3,037 rows).** Not classified here. It already carries a
    `policy_area` assigned by the Library of Congress — a maintained, external,
    authoritative taxonomy. Replacing it with our regex would be strictly worse.
    The useful work there is joining bill numbers parsed from
    `specific_issues_text` to `bill_id`, which is a linkage task, not content
    analysis.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Cut the NAGPRA subset now.** 6,606 notices with a near-exact title-based
   classifier and a genuine three-year surge to lead with. It is the strongest
   product in this corpus and it needs no new sourcing. A concurrent agent is
   already building the rich version (`nagpra_notices.csv`); the index here
   supplies the complete 1994-2026 denominator and the per-year series to
   validate its coverage against.
2. **Fix `land_water` recall** in the lobbying classifier, then redraw a fresh
   audit sample — do not re-score the existing one.
3. **Add a `land_administration` theme** to the FR vocabulary (leasing,
   rights-of-way, HEARTH Act).
4. **Decide about FR body text.** It is the difference between classifying 14%
   and 100% of the corpus. Route it through pull discipline; do not start a
   second poller.
5. **Run a precision pass on the lobbying client universe.** The audit found
   mining companies and utilities in a Native-entity net.
