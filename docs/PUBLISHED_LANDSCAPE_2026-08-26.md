# The Published Landscape — what already exists on Native/tribal economic data

*Swept 2026-08-26. Extends `docs/COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` (2026-08-06), which covers **commercial
competitors** — BGOV, PitchBook, HigherGov, GovWin, Candid, Casino City. This document covers
**published research and public data products**: who has already said the things Cedar Press
is preparing to say.*

**Read this before writing a single line of launch copy.** The project's prime directive is
that it never falsely attributes. That directive does not stop at contract dollars. It extends
to intellectual credit, and this sweep found that Cedar Press is **standing on named prior work
in at least three places** — one of which is load-bearing enough that publishing without the
citation would be a factual error about the project's own origins.

## ⚠ THE HEADLINE FINDING, BEFORE ANYTHING ELSE

**A federal-contracting article dropped two days ago and it is the most important document on
this page.**

> **"Native federal contractors contribute to local economies nationwide"**
> Andrew Huff, Ava LaPlante, Caryn Mohr · Center for Indian Country Development,
> Federal Reserve Bank of Minneapolis · **24 August 2026**
> https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2026/native-federal-contractors-contribute-to-local-economies-nationwide

It is a **synthesis** article: it gathers seven years of CICD federal-contracting research into
one public statement of what is known. That makes it, functionally, the incumbent summary of
the field Cedar Press is about to enter. Its claims are catalogued in §1 below.

And two further findings are larger than the first:

**(a) Cedar Press's entity spine is a derivative of a CICD public data product, and three CICD
publications — including the launch of CICD's tribal-enterprise dataset — are co-authored by
Cedar Press's own owner.** Details in §2, §1.5 and §9. This is not a problem. It is a provenance
that must be *stated*, because it is currently documented only in `AGENTS.md`, an internal file,
and nowhere a reader will ever see it.

**(b) CICD already holds a tribal-enterprise dataset and publishes "aggregated and tribally
anonymized results only," by written policy.** It is not that nobody could publish entity-level
Native economic data. It is that the incumbent built it, staked the "most comprehensive" claim in
public, and chose not to name entities on data-sovereignty grounds. **Cedar Press's launch must
answer that choice rather than walk past it.** §1.5 and §9 item 6.

**(c) The FAADS pre-FY2007 claim is half wrong.** The **0.0% measurement is original and
unclaimed**. The **boundary is not** — it is the plain text of FFATA §2, *"The website shall include
data for fiscal year 2007, and each fiscal year thereafter."* The current framing in
`COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` §0 Finding 5 must be withdrawn and replaced. Wording supplied in §7.

**(d) The sweep also produced a NEW Cedar Press original, measured today.** Tribal governments have
**no classification in the Census Bureau's *Government Finance and Employment Classification
Manual*** — across 463 pages, the words `tribal`, `tribe`, `Native` and `reservation` appear
**0 times**. The United States runs a census of its governments and does not count tribal
governments in it. It is reproducible in one command, needs no statutory caveat, and is a better
opening line than any dollar total. §8.6.

**Bottom line: no Cedar Press claim was found flatly REDUNDANT. But seven planned stories are
already taken (§6), fourteen claims must now be cited rather than originated (§5), one framing must
be withdrawn (§7), the word "first" must come off the enterprise-crosswalk claim (§8B.1), and two
headline counts — 784 gaming facilities and $310.01B contracting — must be reconciled against
published federal figures before they appear anywhere (§8.1, §6). Against that, three genuine gaps
survive intact and two original findings are in hand. The gaps are ranked at the head of §4.**

### 1.2 The methodological disclosure that decides Cedar Press's position

This paragraph is the most commercially significant sentence CICD has ever published about its
own data, and it is in the 2026-08-24 article:

> *"Early analyses drew on **point-in-time data acquired from a vendor**. That dataset lists more
> than 50,000 government contracts awarded to Native entities… **More recent analyses leverage
> continually updated public data available from USAspending, which identify businesses that are
> tribally owned, ANC-owned, and NHO-owned.**"*

Unpacked, from the 2022 appendix (§1.3), this says:

1. **The rich CICD dataset is BGOV-sourced, hand-adjudicated, and frozen at 2021.**
2. **CICD's forward-looking method is the USAspending self-certification flags.**

That second half is the whole competitive picture. `COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` §2 already measures
what a flag-based method costs, on Cedar Press's own files:

- **$53.0B of $79.4B (66.7%)** of tier-A prime obligations flow through vendors whose legal name
  shares **no token** with the owning entity's name.
- The two Native-specific set-asides (Buy Indian + Indian Business) are **0.5% of the dollars**.
- FPDS socioeconomic flags found **241 contracting tribes against 588 recognized entities**.
- **A quarter of the money (25.1%, $19.96B) arrives with no set-aside reported at all.**

So the honest statement of the gap is narrow, true, and defensible: **CICD's 2021 dataset and
Cedar Press's crosswalk are the same *kind* of asset — hand-verified ownership attribution — and
CICD has stopped maintaining theirs in favour of a flag-based method whose undercount Cedar Press
can quantify.** That is the entire commercial opening, and it must be argued as a *methods*
point, never as a claim that CICD lacks data or rigour.

### 1.3 The CICD dataset appendix — read it before claiming any method as novel

From *"Federal contracting's expanding revenue role in Indian Country"*, 21 December 2022
(**Larry Chavis, Matthew Gregg, Elijah Moreno**),
https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2022/federal-contractings-expanding-revenue-role-in-indian-country

> *"The prime dollar amount of each federal contract awarded to each Native entity comes from
> **Bloomberg Government (BGOV)** … Most important for CICD's research purposes, **the name of the
> parent vendor** (i.e., the ultimate owner of the business that was awarded the contract) is also
> included."*

> *"…they matched unmatched companies to a Native entity by **linking the Commercial and Government
> Entity (CAGE) code** of the performing vendor to a Native entity. … If the parent vendor's Native
> entity ownership remained unclear, CICD team members **checked the company's website and did
> additional web searching** to verify Native entity ownership. **For every contract observed, they
> provide documentation on how the link to the Native entity was verified.**"*

> *"This rigorous process … resulted in a dataset of **50,167 unique contracts** obtained from 1981
> … through 2021."*

**Subcontracts came from HigherGov**: *"CICD team members provided HigherGov a dataset of all the
CAGE codes they verified as linked to Native entities, and HigherGov matched these CAGE codes to
associated subcontracts."*

And the limitation, stated by CICD itself:

> *"the dataset could be missing some Native-entity-owned enterprises and their associated prime or
> subcontracts if CICD was unable to match those contracts with an identified Native entity. Thus,
> **CICD is presenting lower-bound estimates**."*

**What this means for Cedar Press.** CAGE-first linkage, parent-vendor observation, per-record
verification documentation, and hand adjudication of ownership are **all published CICD method**,
in a peer-visible Federal Reserve article, in 2022. Cedar Press's tier/rationale/authority model
is a *more formal* instance of it. **Cedar Press must not describe per-record attribution
provenance as an invention.** It is an inheritance, and saying so costs nothing and buys
credibility with the exact audience that will check.

### 1.5 **NEED — the Native Entity Enterprises Dataset.** The finding that most changes Cedar Press's position

CICD does not only hold an entity crosswalk. It holds an **enterprise** dataset, and it says so in
the strongest available terms.

> **"New data showcase the breadth of tribally owned businesses"**
> **Ava LaPlante, Elijah Moreno (Cornell University), Vanessa Palmer** · CICD · **15 April 2025**
> https://www.minneapolisfed.org/article/2025/new-data-showcase-the-breadth-of-tribally-owned-businesses

> *"The Native Entity Enterprises Dataset (NEED) is an ambitious effort that aims to compile a
> comprehensive list of businesses owned by Native entities — that is, tribes, Alaska Native
> Corporations (ANCs), and Native Hawaiian Organizations (NHOs)."*

> *"we've identified **5,559 unique establishments owned by 344 federally recognized tribes**."*

> *"it is, **to our knowledge, the most comprehensive dataset of tribally owned enterprises yet
> compiled**."*

**Measured facts about NEED**, from the launch article and the 2026-04-30 appendix:

| | |
|---|---|

**And here is the line that decides Cedar Press's ethical and strategic position:**

> *"**We present aggregated and tribally anonymized results only**, in keeping with our
> [Principles for Research and Data Use](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/indiancountry/about-us/principles-for-research-and-data-use)."*

**CICD is not unable to publish entity-level tribal enterprise data. It has the data and has
decided not to.** Its Principles (last updated March 2025) state:

> *"CICD understands that it is **the right of Native nations to govern the collection, ownership,
> and application of their respective data**."*

There is also a hard licensing constraint reinforcing the choice: NEED's attributes come from
**NETS, a subscription source** — the same structural bind Cedar Press has with Casino City and
D&B Open Data. CICD likely *could not* republish NEED at establishment level even if it wanted to.

**What this means, stated without spin.** Cedar Press's claim to be first to publish
entity-level Native economic data is **true**. But the space is not empty because nobody could
fill it. It is empty because **the incumbent research institution built the dataset, staked the
"most comprehensive" claim in public, and then deliberately withheld entity identification on
data-sovereignty grounds.** Any launch that presents this space as merely *undiscovered* will be
read as either uninformed or evasive by the exact audience Cedar Press needs — and one of NEED's
three named authors is Cedar Press's own owner.

Cedar Press has a real answer available, and it must be written before launch, not after:
**federal contracts, federal assistance, LDA filings, compacts, Single Audits and FERC dockets are
public records of public money, and the harm Cedar Press corrects is a false record** — Oneida NY
and Oneida WI with $716M mis-split between them, Salt River Project's $28.7M booked to Salt River
Pima-Maricopa. Attribution done properly serves the entity described. That argument is strong.
It is not self-evident, and it does not survive being left unstated. See §9.

`docs/COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` Appendix B says *"The 687 figure is the NEID backbone."* It is

will reasonably conclude Cedar Press invented the scheme.

### What the CICD crosswalk contains — and the clean line it draws

All 37 columns: `neid_prefix, neid_mid, neid_end, neid_suffix, neid_full, entity_type,
entity_namefull, entity_namekey, entity_namedetail, entity_lat, entity_long, entity_city,
entity_state, entity_county, entity_countygeoid, entity_zcta20, entity_web, fedreg_type,
fedreg_namefull, fedreg_namekey, fedreg_nameaka, fedreg_nameprev, biaosg_selfgov,

biaofa_peffdate, biaofa_pweb, id_bia, id_epa`

**Not one is a UEI, CAGE, EIN, DUNS, or any other commercial identifier.** The only IDs it carries
are `id_bia` and `id_epa` — government administrative keys. Its scope is *government-recognized*
entities only: **no ANC village corporations, no NHOs, no tribal enterprises, no subsidiaries.**

That is the precise, defensible boundary:

> **CICD published the government-entity identity layer. Cedar Press adds the commercial-identity
> layer — UEI/CAGE/EIN → owning entity — plus the subsidiary ownership graph. CICD's crosswalk
> does not contain, and does not attempt, either one.**

Cedar Press's extension is real and large: 687 → **1,310**, adding 185 BIE schools, 173 ANC village
corps, 64 Native CDFIs, 55 intertribal orgs, 43 UIOs, 37 TCUs, 31 NHOs, 29 Native financial
institutions, 6 ANCSA group corps. **Claim the extension. Cite the backbone.**

## 7. The FAADS pre-FY2007 finding — status

`COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` §0 Finding 5 records this as a Cedar Press original: *per-entity federal

### VERDICT: the measurement is original. **The boundary is statute, and the current framing must be withdrawn.**

**No prior art exists for the rate.** No GAO, CRS, OMB, Treasury or Census document reached in this
sweep states that FAADS carries a recipient identifier on 0.0% of FY2001–06 rows. That finding is
Cedar Press's.

**But "per-entity federal assistance cannot begin before FY2007" is the plain text of the enabling
statute**, and publishing it as a discovery would be the single most damaging credibility error

> *"The website shall include data for **fiscal year 2007, and each fiscal year thereafter**."*
> …and must carry *"a **unique identifier of the entity receiving the award** and of the parent entity."*

FY2001–06 FAADS has no recipient identifier **because no law required one.**

**6. Answer the entity-identification question before launch, in public. This is now the single
most important item in this document after the citation obligations.**

The question is no longer abstract. It has a named, dated, institutional precedent pointing the
other way:

- CICD holds NEED — 5,559 establishments across 344 tribes — and publishes **"aggregated and
  tribally anonymized results only."**
- Its Principles for Research and Data Use (March 2025) state that **"it is the right of Native
  nations to govern the collection, ownership, and application of their respective data."**
- NNI and GIDA maintain a 20+ item Indigenous Data Sovereignty literature, at the same institution
  as the Kalt/Akee economics, arguing the same thing under the CARE principles.
- One of NEED's three named authors is Cedar Press's owner.

So the first serious question Cedar Press will be asked is not *"is your data good?"* It is
**"the Minneapolis Fed decided not to name tribes in exactly this analysis — why are you naming
them, and why are you charging for it?"** Nothing currently in `docs/` answers that.

The answer exists and is defensible. Write it down, publish it, and make it specific:

- **The sources are different in kind.** NEED's identifying attributes come from NETS, a
  *subscription commercial establishment database*, and from tribes' own websites. Cedar Press's
  attributions rest on **federal records of federal money** — FPDS, FABS, Senate LDA, BIA compacts,
  Single Audits, FERC dockets, IRS 990s — every one already public and already naming the recipient.
  Cedar Press does not disclose anything the federal government has not already disclosed. **It
  corrects who the disclosure belongs to.**
- **The harm being addressed is misattribution, and it runs against tribes.** $716M mis-split
  between Oneida NY and Oneida WI. Salt River Project's $28.7M booked to Salt River Pima-Maricopa.
  Cherokee General Corp, which is Doyon-owned, read as Cherokee Nation. A name-matching market
  does not merely under-count Native entities — **it assigns one nation's revenue to another.**
  Anonymization does not fix that. Correct attribution does.
- **Say what Cedar Press will not do.** A stated refusal is worth more than a stated permission.
  Candidates, all supported by material already in the build: no publication of tier-B or below;
  no per-entity assistance before FY2007; no revenue figure called "revenue" unless it is one of
  the 21% that are reported; **NAGPRA never in a commercial alert stream** (point 7); a named
  correction-and-withdrawal route for any entity that disputes a row; and a standing offer that
  any entity may obtain its own complete Cedar Press record free.
- **Offer review, do not merely assert good faith.** CICD's Principles commit to giving data
  providers *"opportunities to provide feedback on research."* Cedar Press's own review queue
  (`web_claude/entity_reconciliation.html`, 493 observations) is already the machinery for this.
  Turning it outward — letting an entity see and contest its own attributions before publication —
  converts the biggest reputational risk in the project into its strongest trust claim.

A governance posture written in advance reads as principle. The same posture written afterwards
reads as damage control. This one has a two-day-old article and a three-author byline attached to
it, and it will be found.

**7. Route NAGPRA separately, or not at all.** 6,729 repatriation notices are the most sensitive
material in the build. `COMPETITIVE_POSITION.md` §3.3 already warns that the obvious
implementation ships *"5 new federal actions for Navajo Nation this week"* where four are ancestral
remains notices. Under the data-sovereignty frame in point 6, this is the single most likely
source of serious reputational damage in the entire product. **Decide it deliberately and record
the decision.**

## OWNER RULING 2026-08-26 — the CICD crosswalk is an INPUT, not a blocker

The scan flagged the entity spine's derivation from CICD's Native Entity
Connector Crosswalk (687 entities, prefixes TRBF/AKNF/TRBS/CNSF/ANRC/SGVF/CNSS)
as an attribution risk. **Elijah has ruled it is not.** His reasoning, recorded
because it is correct and future agents should not re-open it:

1. **The entities are public facts.** Federally recognized tribes come from the
   Federal Register list; ANCSA corporations from statute. Anyone can build the
   same crosswalk from the same sources. A directory of public entities is not
   an original work.
2. **Cedar Press has its own identification system** and has more than doubled
   the universe: **687 → 1,489**, adding classes CICD's file does not carry at
   all — 210 Native Hawaiian Organizations, 64 state-recognized tribes, 185 BIE
   schools, 64 Native CDFIs, 55 intertribal organizations, 43 urban Indian
   organizations, 37 tribal colleges. There is no NHO layer in the CICD prefix
   set.
3. **NEED is not accessible.** CICD publishes *"aggregated and tribally
   anonymized results only"* by written policy. An entity-level product does not
   compete with a dataset nobody can query at entity level.

**What still holds:** put one line on the methods page crediting the CICD
crosswalk as an input. Elijah is a co-author on three CICD publications, which
makes the acknowledgment ordinary scholarly practice rather than a concession,
and it removes the criticism entirely. This is a courtesy and a positioning
move, not a legal or ethical requirement.

**What does NOT survive this ruling** — still must be fixed before publication:
the FAADS framing. The 0.0% recipient-identifier measurement FY2001-06 is
original and unclaimed and may ship. The claim that *the federal government does
not document the boundary* may NOT — FFATA §2 states *"The website shall include
data for fiscal year 2007, and each fiscal year thereafter."* Measurement keeps,
framing goes.
