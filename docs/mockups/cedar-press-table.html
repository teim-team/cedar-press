What changed, and why

  1. Seven columns become five. The arrow column goes (the whole row opens the record, which is what a reader tries first anyway), entity type joins the identifier line under the name, and Source becomes the ↑↗ at the end of the record it belongs to.
  2. Nothing is clipped mid-word. Today the entity name, the alias line, the observation and the amount’s basis all end in an ellipsis or run off the right edge. Four truncations per row is the reason the table reads as dense: the eye keeps stopping at words that do not finish. Records wrap instead.
  3. The collection carries its mark. The door’s rail is legible because twelve marks are faster to tell apart than twelve teal links. In a table that mixes collections in one view, the mark is doing more work than it does on the door.
  4. The identifier is the second line, in teal, always. It is the one thing on the row no other product has, and today it is a grey sub-line indistinguishable from the alias beneath it. Where a record has no identifier, the row says what that means rather than printing an em dash in four columns.
  5. A caption band, not a paragraph. What is in view and how much of it moves into the mono band the door already uses, so the toolbar is controls and the band is state.
