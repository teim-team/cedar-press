## 2. The two rules, and how they are enforced in code rather than in prose

### 2.1 A cap is an authorisation, never a count

Every device, table and facility cap is stamped
`measurement_type = AUTHORIZED_MAXIMUM`. The build asserts at startup that
`cedar_domain.may_promote(AUTHORIZED_MAXIMUM, ACTIVE_FLOOR_COUNT)` is `False`, and
asserts again on every row that no cap carries `ACTIVE_FLOOR_COUNT`. A property
authorised for 2,500 devices may operate 900; the file cannot express the
confusion.

### 2.2 Compact-derived revenue is exact arithmetic only if the concept survives

Three separate columns carry the three separate facts, and they must be read
together:

- **`revenue_concept`** — the compact's own words for what the rate applies to
  (`net win`, `Class III Net Win`, `Gross Gaming Revenue`, `adjusted gross
  gaming receipts`, …). This is copied verbatim and never generalised. If the
  formula covers only Class III electronic net win, the quotient is Class III
  electronic net win — **not total casino revenue**.
- **`base_scope`** — whether the compact binds the revenue base to a single
  property, or to the tribe's gaming as a whole.
- **`formula_invertibility`** — whether a payment divided by the rate uniquely
  recovers the revenue amount.

`INVERTIBLE_FLAT_RATE` requires exactly one distinct rate, no progressive
schedule, no minimum-payment floor and no per-device component in the same
instrument. Anything else is `NOT_INVERTIBLE` and carries a **`bound_basis`**
naming what blocks the inversion. **A factual bound is not a confidence
interval**; no interval is computed anywhere in this build, and no modelled
property revenue is produced.

`revenue_evidence_class` follows from those, using the shared
`cedar_domain.REVENUE_EVIDENCE` vocabulary. It reaches
`EXACT_DERIVED_PROPERTY_REVENUE` only where the base is facility-bound **and**
the formula inverts. Otherwise it is `BOUNDED_DERIVED_REVENUE` or, in the
ordinary case, `TRIBE_LEVEL_REVENUE` — because a compact obligation is a
tribe-level obligation and a tribe total is not a property revenue figure.

## 3. Four defects found and fixed, each of which would have shipped a wrong number

**(a) A standard-form state compact prints every signatory's allocation inside
every tribe's copy.** Arizona's 2003 and 2021 instruments read
*"If the Tribe is the Ak-Chin Indian Community, the Tribe may operate a maximum
of one (1) Gaming Facility…"* — inside the Yavapai-Apache compact, the Cocopah
compact, and every other. The first pass keyed Ak-Chin's facility cap to twelve
different tribes. A rejection guard now refuses any clause conditioned on a
tribe whose name shares no distinctive token with the compact's own tribe.
Facility-cap rows fell from 67 to 1 on the 25-version test slice, and **237
candidates corpus-wide are now in the review file** instead of the dataset. The
guard only ever *rejects*; it never links a term to the tribe it names.

**(b) "The operation of Gaming Devices" is not "each gaming facility".**
California's revenue-share clause reads *"six percent (6.0 %) of its Net Win
from the operation of Gaming Devices"*. A first-cut facility test matched
"the … operation" and promoted 44 California rate rows to
`EXACT_DERIVED_PROPERTY_REVENUE` — property-level revenue evidence built on a
tribe-level base. The base test now requires a **distributive** determiner
(`each` / `any` / `every` / `per`) and a noun that is a *place*. What survives is
the real thing: Michigan's *"two percent (2%) of the net win at each casino"*,
which genuinely inverts to that casino's net win.

**(c) A progressive schedule with spelled-out brackets read as a flat rate.**
New Mexico's compacts say *"Three Percent (3%) of the first Four Million Dollars
($4,000,000) of net win at each Gaming Facility … and Five Percent (5%) of the
net win over the first Four Million Dollars"*. Both digit-anchored tier patterns
missed the worded bracket, so the instrument looked like a single flat rate and
was marked `INVERTIBLE_FLAT_RATE` — which would have licensed exact division
against a two-bracket schedule. This is the most dangerous class of error in the
whole build, because the output looks like a fact. A third tier pattern now
accepts any bracket phrase, and those compacts correctly resolve to
`BOUNDED_DERIVED_REVENUE`.

**(d) The Secretary's approval letter is not the compact.** The letter bundled
at the front of many BIA PDFs *describes* the instrument, sometimes in numbers
that do not survive contact with the text (one 1997 letter states the compact
does **not** provide substantial exclusivity). Approval-letter pages are zoned
separately, marked `is_instrument_language = no`, and **excluded from the
invertibility decision** — they carry
`formula_invertibility = NOT_APPLICABLE_APPROVAL_LETTER` and no
`revenue_evidence_class`.

A fifth, purely mechanical defect is worth recording because it changed the
build from feasible to infeasible: the table-of-contents guard recomputed
page-level statistics inside every match test. On a 600 KB instrument with
thousands of candidates that is a quadratic scan, and it cost **14 seconds on a
single document**. Hoisting the page statistics out of the loop cut the same
document to 0.8 s.

## 4. Reporting obligations — the discovery mechanism

`compact_required_reports.csv` records every clause that requires a report,
statement, certification, accounting or audit to be filed, with the recipient
agency as the compact names it, the frequency, the fields the clause enumerates,
and whether the report covers each facility separately or the tribe as a whole.

**A reporting obligation is a map to a series a state agency holds.** A compact
ordering a quarterly net-win certification is telling us a state agency has a
quarterly net-win series, whether or not anyone has ever asked for it.

4,121 obligations were recorded. 1,466 name a state body as recipient, 1,029 a
tribal gaming agency, 22 a federal one, and 1,604 name no recipient inside the
clause. On cadence: 1,062 annual, 394 quarterly, 233 monthly, 20 weekly, 345 on
request, and a large deadline-only class (`within_10_days` 233,
`within_30_days` 136, `within_60_days` 104 and others). The most-named
recipients are the generic "State Gaming Agency" (1,181) and "Tribal Gaming
Agency" (674), then Gaming Control Board (84), Department of Revenue (23) and
the NIGC (22).

The fields those reports must carry are the reason to chase them: licences
(665), employees (414), contributions (348), **net win (300)**, financial
statements (278), hold (88), amounts wagered (58), drop (52), device counts (48)
and handle (39).

What the compact can and cannot tell us about disclosure is kept strictly
separate:

- **`public_availability`** is what the reporting clause *itself* says
  (`CONFIDENTIAL_PER_COMPACT` / `PUBLIC_PER_COMPACT` / `NOT_STATED_IN_COMPACT`).
- **`version_has_confidentiality_provision`** records that the same instrument
  has a confidentiality section *somewhere else*. It is a separate column
  deliberately: a general confidentiality clause does not automatically reach
  the revenue-sharing certification, and folding the two together would suppress
  public series that in fact exist.
- Neither column claims the agency **does** publish. That is a different
  question, answered below by files we have actually read.

## 6. Tiering, entity keying and the review queue

Every term and report row is `confidence_tier = B`. These are algorithmic
extractions with receipts, not human rulings, and spec 10.1 lands automated
results at B pending review. **Nothing here publishes without a review pass.**

Entity keying is a separate fact and is carried in its own columns
(`entity_match_method`, `entity_tier`), inherited from `compacts.csv`, which had

## 7. Known limits

- **The 88 original instruments that yielded nothing** need a hand read before
  anyone concludes they carry no terms.
- **Recall is deliberately below precision throughout.** Every guard in section 3
  removes true positives along with false ones; that trade was made on purpose
  and each guard's reason is recorded in the code beside it.
- **`gaming_types_authorized` enumerates games from the retained quote.** The
  15c pilot found the authorising section reliably but did not enumerate; the
  enumeration added here is checkable against the quote on every row, and games
  named outside the quoted window are not captured.
- **A compact term is what the compact says, not what happened.** An authorised
  cap does not mean the devices exist, a revenue-share rate does not mean a
  payment was made, and an amendment approved does not mean it was implemented.
- **`report_subject_level` is almost always `unspecified`** (4,115 of 4,121).
  The facility-versus-tribe test only fires on explicit language, and compact
  reporting clauses rarely carry it. Do not read `unspecified` as "tribe-level".
- **`public_availability` is `NOT_STATED_IN_COMPACT` for 3,872 of 4,121
  clauses.** 165 clauses say confidential and 84 say public. The silence is
  real: most compacts simply do not address disclosure where they impose the
  report.
- **`compact_terms.csv` (15e) and `compact_structured_terms.csv` (95) overlap on
  four concepts** — device caps, revenue-share rate, revenue-share base, local
  share. They were built by different passes with different guards. Treat a
  disagreement between them as a finding to adjudicate, not as a duplicate to
  deduplicate.
