# The shard program — working the master list, not just the datasets

*Written 2026-09-01. Live doc. Status numbers live in `docs/SHARD_COVERAGE.md`,
which is generated by `code/528_shard_consolidate.py` — do not hand-edit counts
into this file.*

## Why this exists

Cedar's acquisition work had been organized **by dataset**: one agent for gaming,
one for lobbying, one for natural resources. That misses two things the owner
named on 2026-09-01:

1. **It re-visits the same tribe four times.** Owner: *"makes it easier to
   coordinate, like, oh, we scrape this tribe's casino website and check the
   TERO."* One visit per entity, take everything, is strictly cheaper.
2. **It never touches most of the master list.** Cedar holds **1,555 entities**
   and the dataset-shaped work only ever reached the 577 federally recognized
   governments. Owner: *"we should get more use out of our master list."*

So acquisition is now sharded **by entity**, across the whole register.

## The blocker that started it

`cedar_identity_register.csv` has an `entity_website` column. On 2026-09-01 it
was populated for **0 of 1,555 entities**, and only 192 of 787 gaming facilities
carried any URL. There was no scrape map. Every "just scrape the tribal sites"
plan had been resting on a map that did not exist.

## The nine shards

| shard | slice | n | headline deliverable beyond the map |
|---|---|---:|---|
| A | tribal governments, gaming slice 1 | 71 | TERO lists, casino detail |
| B | tribal governments, gaming slice 2 | 71 | TERO lists, casino detail |
| C | tribal governments, gaming slice 3 | 71 | TERO lists, casino detail |
| D | gaming slice 4, then non-gaming tribes | 71+ | reaches tribes with no casino at all |
| E | Alaska Native corporations | 191 | **subsidiary edges — corporate hierarchy ground truth** |
| F | intertribal, urban Indian, consortia, constituency | 153 | **membership rosters** |
| G | BIE schools, tribal colleges, CDFIs, financial institutions | 315 | **registry IDs: NCES, IPEDS, CDFI Fund, NCUA, FDIC** |
| H | Native Hawaiian orgs, state-recognized tribes, individual businesses | 319 | recognition instruments, NHO 8(a) subsidiaries |
| I | Native nonprofits | 12,764 | evidence for the 412 tier-A rows awaiting a ruling |

Shards A–H partition the 1,555-entity register. Shard I works `np_orgs.csv`,
which is **not in the register at all** — see the open question below.

## Why shard E is the one to watch

The owner's standing position is that a correct tribal corporate hierarchy is
*"impossible to establish outside of tribes doing it for you"* — SAM's reported
ultimate parent is frequently the holding company (Ho-Chunk Inc.) rather than
the government (Winnebago Tribe), and names like *Arctic Slope Federal Services*
give no hint of their owner.

ANCs resolve this from the opposite direction: **they publish their own
subsidiary lists.** An "Our Companies" page is ownership asserted by the parent
itself, which is a stronger evidence class than anything derivable from SAM, and
it is the closest thing available to tribes doing it for us. Shard E chases these
to depth 2, because the second layer is where the spiderweb actually lives.

## What every shard was told

- **Scrape and download, don't survey.** Raw captures plus normalized JSONL into
  `data/staging/`. A list of URLs is not the deliverable.
- **Take what the page sends, not just what it shows** — see
  `docs/HIDDEN_DATA_TECHNIQUES.md`. A directory rendering 10 of 340 rows usually
  ships all 340 in a JSON blob or an API the page itself calls. On tribal sites
  the WordPress media library (`/wp-json/wp/v2/media`) and ArcGIS
  `FeatureServer` endpoints are the two highest-yield sources, and one API call
  is *gentler* on a small server than thirty page fetches.
- **Newsletters, everywhere.** Owner: tribal newsletters are where deals surface
  before any federal filing. Record the channel, format, archive depth and
  cadence; sample 3 recent issues; do **not** harvest full archives yet.
- **Do not give up on a blocked source.** Escalation ladder below.
- **Never fabricate.** An entity with no findable site gets a row saying so.
  Missing coverage is expandable; a wrong attribution is not.
- **Never mint, never resolve identity, never touch the spine.** Candidate
  matches are recorded with a confidence; `503`/`510` own resolution.

## The alternative-route ladder

Owner, 2026-09-01: *"make sure you don't give up if you can't access or scrape
something and you find alternative routes to it."* A 403, a JS-only page or a
dead link is the start of the search:

1. Wayback Machine — a vendor list or newsletter archive removed in a redesign
   is very often still there
2. the PDF behind the page (`filetype:pdf`, `/uploads/`, `/wp-content/`)
3. Facebook or a Google Sites / Squarespace subdomain, where smaller
   organizations actually publish
4. the separate site — the tribal newspaper, the TERO office, the subsidiary
5. the regulator: state gaming commission, state business registry, CDFI Fund,
   NCES/IPEDS, SBA DSBS, ProPublica Nonprofit Explorer
6. news coverage of the transaction

Shards record **which rung worked** in `evidence`, so the next agent skips the
rungs that failed.

**This ladder is for sources that are BROKEN, never for sources that REFUSED.**
`source_terms_status = TERMS_STATED_RESTRICTIVE` — Confederated Colville and
CTUIR among them — stays excluded by every route including Wayback. Terms are a
decision the publisher made, not an obstacle to route around.

## Consolidation

`code/528_shard_consolidate.py` merges all nine shard maps into
`data/staging/cedar_web_map.csv` and writes `docs/SHARD_COVERAGE.md`. It is safe
to run mid-flight; a missing shard file reports as `NOT_STARTED`, not an error.

It deliberately does **not** write to the spine. Promoting a harvested website to
`entity_website` is an assertion and goes through `510` with a source and a
confidence.

The coverage ledger separates three states that are easy to conflate, and the
distinction is the whole point:

- **with a URL** — found
- **touched, none found** — attempted, genuinely has no web presence
- **untouched** — nobody has tried

Only the third is a gap in our effort, and without the split it is invisible.

## Open questions for the owner

**1. Should nonprofits be minted into the master list?** The register's 17
classes contain no nonprofit class. `np_orgs.csv` holds 12,764 organizations of
which **1,423 (11.1%) carry a `cedar_uid`**; ~11,300 sit in a Cedar dataset with
no presence in the entity layer they are meant to key to. Minting all of them
would nearly nine-times the register and import IRS-classification uncertainty
into the identity spine. Not minting leaves dataset 6 permanently unable to
satisfy the hub-and-spoke contract in ADR-009. Shard I is gathering the evidence
and will recommend; the decision is the owner's.

**2. Newsletters as dataset 14.** Owner: *"even just keeping track of your
newsletters could be a potential different dataset down the road with, like, the
content."* Shards are mapping channels now and sampling for business content, so
the feasibility evidence will exist before anything is committed. Nothing is
being built for this yet.

## Deadline

The owner set **end of October 2026** for built datasets, and said plainly he is
not confident it will happen. The shard program is the bet: parallel entity-wise
acquisition across the whole master list, rather than sequential dataset-wise
passes that were leaving two thirds of Cedar's entities untouched.
